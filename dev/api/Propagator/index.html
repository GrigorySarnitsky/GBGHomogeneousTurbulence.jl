Propagator

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator — Module
Propagator

This module holds the interchangeable time-marching schemes, concrete AbstractIntegrator subtypes; a run chooses one as the field_integration_scheme of its Simulation. All are of the integrating-factor family: the stiff diffusive part is advanced exactly and the nonlinear term explicitly by a Runge–Kutta stage combination.

  • AbstractIntegrator — the scheme supertype a run's field_integration_scheme is chosen from.
  • IFLowStorageRK3, IFLowStorageRK4, IFSSPRK3, IFHeun — the schemes (RK3 low-storage, the workhorse; the Carpenter–Kennedy fourth-order sibling at the same two field-sized registers, for stability-limited runs; the Shu–Osher SSPRK3 as arranged in the DNS code TurTLE, for code-to-code comparison; IFHeun a two-stage Heun — cheaper in transforms, dearer by one register per field, and imaginary-axis unstable).
  • IntegratorBuffers, allocate_buffers — the preallocated per-step scratch.
  • step! — advance the coupled velocity and passive scalar one timestep.
  • deposit! — close the iteration: recover the advanced state's observables into the run's cache.
  • recommended_cfl — a scheme's advective-CFL ceiling.

Integrator schemes

This section documents the integrating-factor schemes: the third-order low-storage RK3; the fourth-order low-storage RK4 of Carpenter and Kennedy, five stages in the same two field-sized registers with a wider imaginary-axis stability limit; the third-order Shu–Osher SSPRK3 arranged as in the reference DNS code TurTLE, for code-to-code comparison at the same cost; then the second-order Heun, imaginary-axis unstable and cheaper in transforms but dearer in memory.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.AbstractIntegrator — Type
AbstractIntegrator

Supertype of the time-marching schemes: IFLowStorageRK3 (the default), IFLowStorageRK4, IFSSPRK3 and IFHeun. A scheme implements step!(scheme, state, generator, domain, buffers, cache, dt), advancing the state's velocity $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ and scalar $\hat\varphi$ (both in spectral representation) by one timestep in place, and may specialize recommended_cfl.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.IFLowStorageRK3 — Type
IFLowStorageRK3()

Integrating-factor third-order low-storage Runge–Kutta of the Williamson (1980) 2N-array family. The general-purpose workhorse: third-order and stable on the imaginary axis (limit $\sqrt{3}$), unlike IFHeun.

The diffusive integrating factor $\mathcal{E}_D(\delta t)$ over a substep interval $\delta t$ ($D = \nu$ for the velocity, $\varkappa = \nu/\mathrm{Sc}$ for the scalar) takes its parameters from the generator's velocity_integrating_factor / scalar_integrating_factor and is applied per mode by GBGPeriodicFields' fused exponential convolution, never built as a field. With $\widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}$ each field's right-hand side, the step carries a state $\hat q$ and a single accumulator $\boldsymbol{h}$ through three stages $i = 1,2,3$,

\[\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{h} &\leftarrow A_i \boldsymbol{h} + \Delta t \widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}(\hat q), \\ \hat q &\leftarrow \hat q + B_i \boldsymbol{h}, \\ \hat q &\leftarrow \mathcal{E}_D(d_i\Delta t) \hat q, \\ \boldsymbol{h} &\leftarrow \mathcal{E}_D(d_i\Delta t) \boldsymbol{h}, \end{aligned}\]

(the accumulator's transport by the integrating factor is skipped at the last stage, where nothing reads it again),

with stage coefficients $A_i = (0, -5/9, -153/128)$, $B = (1/3, 15/16, 8/15)$, and node increments $d = (1/3, 5/12, 1/4)$ (abscissae $c = (0, 1/3, 3/4)$). The velocity and scalar are advanced in lockstep (the same $A,B,d$), each with its own diffusivity, and any forcing's post-step projection (project_forcing!) is applied after the third stage.

The cost is three nonlinear evaluations per step and — in Williamson's 2N-storage count, whose label is unrelated to the grid's $N$ — two registers per field: the state's own array plus one accumulator (stage1). The IntegratorBuffers a run allocates also holds nonlinear, the register the right-hand-side assembly and its dealiasing pass write through: the 2N count measures the scheme, not the solver it is embedded in.

References

  • Williamson, J. H. (1980). Low-storage Runge–Kutta schemes. J. Comput. Phys. 35, 48–56.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.IFLowStorageRK4 — Type
IFLowStorageRK4()

Integrating-factor fourth-order low-storage Runge–Kutta of Carpenter & Kennedy (1994, Fourth-order 2N-storage Runge–Kutta schemes, NASA TM-109112): five stages in the same Williamson 2N form — and therefore the same two registers per field — as IFLowStorageRK3, advancing the same shared stage loop with Carpenter & Kennedy's coefficients (stated in the technical memorandum as exact rationals; abscissae $c \approx (0, 0.1497, 0.3704, 0.6223, 0.9583)$).

Use it when the run is stability-limited and the transforms dominate: its stability function is $R(z) = \sum_{k \le 4} z^{k}/k! + z^{5}/200$, whose imaginary-axis stability limit is

\[y_s = \sqrt{\tfrac{5(\sqrt{2041} - 5)}{18}} \approx 3.3407,\]

That limit is a factor $1.93$ above the $\sqrt{3}$ of IFLowStorageRK3, and a step makes $5/3$ as many nonlinear evaluations. Every per-stage cost scales with the five stages alike, so a step costs $5/3$ of IFLowStorageRK3's, and a stability-limited run advances $1.93/(5/3) \approx 1.16$ times the physical time per unit compute. Its fourth-order accuracy also removes the field's own time error as the limit on an AdamsBashforth(order = 4) tracer, whose Heun bootstrap still caps its measured convergence near third order.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.IFSSPRK3 — Type
IFSSPRK3()

Integrating-factor strong-stability-preserving third-order Runge–Kutta of Shu & Osher (1988) — the SSPRK3 tableau, in exactly the stage arrangement of TurTLE, the reference pseudospectral solver, so a code-to-code comparison against it shares the time discretization. Choose it for that comparison; for everything else it and IFLowStorageRK3 are interchangeable — same order, same three right-hand-side evaluations, same two registers per field, and the same linear stability (every three-stage third-order explicit Runge–Kutta has stability function $R(z) = 1 + z + z^2/2 + z^3/6$, imaginary-axis limit $\sqrt{3}$), differing only in the nonlinear error constants.

With $\mathcal{E}(\delta t)$ the diffusive integrating factor over a span $\delta t$ (from the generator's velocity_integrating_factor / scalar_integrating_factor; applied per mode by the fused exponential convolution, never built as a field) and $\widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}$ each field's right-hand side, one step of a field $\hat q$ is the Shu–Osher convex-combination form, each term transported to its stage's time:

\[\begin{aligned} \hat q^{(1)} &= \mathcal{E}(\Delta t)\bigl[\hat q_n + \Delta t \widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}(\hat q_n)\bigr], \\ \hat q^{(2)} &= \tfrac{3}{4} \mathcal{E}(\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}) \hat q_n + \tfrac{1}{4} \mathcal{E}(-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2})\bigl[\hat q^{(1)} + \Delta t \widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}(\hat q^{(1)})\bigr], \\ \hat q_{n+1} &= \tfrac{1}{3} \mathcal{E}(\Delta t) \hat q_n + \tfrac{2}{3} \mathcal{E}(\tfrac{\Delta t}{2})\bigl[\hat q^{(2)} + \Delta t \widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}(\hat q^{(2)})\bigr], \end{aligned}\]

with abscissae $c = (0, 1, \tfrac{1}{2})$: the stages step forward to $t_{n+1}$, back to the midpoint, forward again. The velocity and scalar are advanced in lockstep, each with its own diffusivity, and any forcing's post-step projection (project_forcing!) is applied after the third stage.

The cost is three nonlinear evaluations per step and two registers per field — the stage state and the right-hand-side register — the same count as IFLowStorageRK3 (one pointwise pass fewer, since no accumulator is transported by the integrating factor).

The midpoint stage transports backward

At wavenumber magnitude $\kappa$, the $\mathcal{E}(-\Delta t/2)$ factor is $\mathrm{e}^{+\nu\kappa^{2}\Delta t/2}$ — a growing exponential, exact but amplifying at large $\nu\kappa^{2}\Delta t$ before the convex combination cancels it. Under an advective CFL step this exponent is small and the loss is roundoff-level; a run driven with a hand-fixed $\Delta t \gg$ the advective step would amplify high-wavenumber roundoff instead. IFLowStorageRK3's stages only ever transport forward and carry no such term.

References

  • Shu, C.-W. & Osher, S. (1988). Efficient implementation of essentially non-oscillatory shock-capturing schemes. J. Comput. Phys. 77, 439–471.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.IFHeun — Type
IFHeun()

Integrating-factor Heun — the two-stage, second-order Runge–Kutta. With the velocity and scalar integrating factors $\mathcal{E}_\nu$ and $\mathcal{E}_\varkappa$ (the generator's velocity_integrating_factor / scalar_integrating_factor at $D = \nu$ and $D = \varkappa = \nu/\mathrm{Sc}$ over the step $\Delta t$) and $\widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}$ each field's right-hand side, one step of a field $\hat q$ with integrating factor $\mathcal{E}$ is

\[\begin{aligned} \hat q^{*} &= \mathcal{E} (\hat q_n + \Delta t \widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}_1), \\ \hat q_{n+1} &= \mathcal{E} \hat q_n + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}(\mathcal{E} \widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}_1 + \widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}_2), \end{aligned}\]

with $\widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}_1$ at $\hat q_n$ and $\widehat{\boldsymbol{N}}_2$ at the predictor $\hat q^{*}$; velocity and scalar use $\mathcal{E}_\nu$ and $\mathcal{E}_\varkappa$ respectively, then the forcings are applied. A step costs two nonlinear evaluations.

Unstable on the imaginary axis without viscous damping

Every second-order two-stage explicit Runge–Kutta has stability function $R(z) = 1 + z + z^{2}/2$, for which $\lvert R(\mathrm{i}y)\rvert^{2} = 1 + y^{4}/4 > 1$: the scheme is unstable on the imaginary axis, so it is stable only where the viscous damping of the integrating factor dominates. It is adequate for well-resolved, modest-Reynolds-number runs — prefer IFLowStorageRK3 for high Reynolds number or marginal resolution.

Step scratch

This section documents the preallocated per-step working set the scheme writes into, which is built per concrete scheme and sized so a step allocates no field-sized memory.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.IntegratorBuffers — Type
IntegratorBuffers

The preallocated per-step scratch the scheme writes into, sized so a step allocates no field-sized memory. Built per scheme by allocate_buffers(scheme, domain; scalar), which allocates exactly that scheme's working set: an array another scheme would use is left nothing, never allocated.

Each such array is a register: storage a scheme computes one stage into and overwrites at the next, holding nothing a caller should read between steps. The observables cache's slots serve the same purpose mid-iteration — the step computes its stage recoveries into them — which is why a cached field is meaningful only between iterations.

There are deliberately no physical-representation registers here: the substage velocity, vorticity, and scalar recoveries write into the run's ObservablesCache slots, taken as scratch mid-iteration and re-deposited at the iteration's end by deposit! — a slot IS its producer's register. Likewise there is no integrating-factor register: the exact linear subflow is applied by the backend's fused exponential convolution (exponential_convolve!), evaluated per mode from the factor's parameters, so no kernel field is ever built or stored. The right-hand-side assembly scratch (the quadratic products, the phase-shift buffers, any steady forcing rate) lives with the generator, on the ResolvedGenerator — the buffers here are only the scheme's own spectral stage registers.

Fields

  • stage1 — the first Runge–Kutta stage register, allocated by every scheme (the accumulator for IFLowStorageRK3 and IFLowStorageRK4, the stage state for IFSSPRK3, the first stage for IFHeun).
  • nonlinear — the assembled right-hand side; every scheme but IFHeun, where it is nothing.
  • stage2, velocity_predictor_spectral — the second stage register and the substage velocity predictor $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*}$ in spectral representation; IFHeun only, else nothing.
  • scalar — a ScalarBuffers (an internal record; this entry is its full description) for the passive scalar $\varphi$ when scalar = true, else nothing; when nothing, the run evolves no scalar and none of its field-sized buffers are allocated. Its fields mirror the per-scheme breakdown above, sized to the scalar: stage1 (every scheme), stage2 and predictor_spectral (IFHeun's corrector register and predictor state $\hat\varphi^*$), and nonlinear (every scheme but IFHeun).

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.allocate_buffers — Function
allocate_buffers(scheme, domain; scalar = false) -> IntegratorBuffers
allocate_buffers(scheme, tracers, cache) -> ParticleIntegratorBuffers

Allocate the per-step registers a scheme needs, dispatching on the concrete scheme so it allocates exactly its working set — never a register another scheme would use. A scheme with no method here is a hard error; there is no over-allocating fallback. The constructor of a Simulation calls this for you.

Arguments

  • scheme — the concrete scheme dispatched on: an AbstractIntegrator in the field form, an AbstractParticleIntegrator in the particle form.
  • domain — field form: the FixedDomain, which fixes the grid, precision, and storage backend of every register.
  • scalar = false — field form: true adds the nested ScalarBuffers, likewise sized to the scheme.
  • tracers — particle form: the tracer set the registers are sized to.
  • cache — particle form: the ObservablesCache whose velocity-at-particles slot the scheme consumes and — for a multistep scheme — whose sample history it integrates over.

The first form builds the IntegratorBuffers the scheme needs: IFLowStorageRK3, IFLowStorageRK4 and IFSSPRK3 allocate stage1 and nonlinear; IFHeun allocates stage1, stage2, and velocity_predictor_spectral. The second form builds the ParticleIntegratorBuffers. One generic covers both because it is one concept, and because every side keeps the same separation: the evolving state holds no scheme's scratch.

Time stepping

This section documents one in-place step of the coupled velocity and passive scalar together through the shared RK stages, and the deposit that closes the iteration.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.step! — Function
step!(scheme, state, generator, domain, buffers, cache, dt)

Advance the velocity $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ and passive scalar $\hat\varphi$ held in state by one timestep dt in place. Dispatches on the concrete scheme type (the schemes are catalogued under AbstractIntegrator).

Arguments

  • scheme — the stateless dispatch tag naming the scheme.
  • state — the ResolvedFields this call mutates: the fields in spectral representation.
  • generator — the ResolvedGenerator: the equations of motion, their conditions, and their assembly scratch.
  • domain — the FixedDomain.
  • buffers — the scheme's IntegratorBuffers.
  • cache — the run's ObservablesCache.
  • dt — the timestep.

A scheme does only stage arithmetic: it opens the step with the generator's prepare!, evaluates the right-hand sides and exact linear factors through the generator's velocity_rhs!, scalar_rhs! and integrating-factor methods at each stage, and closes with the generator's finalize! — it never assembles the physics itself.

The first stage reads the deposited velocity, vorticity, and scalar in physical representation from cache — the values of the instant between iterations, deposited by the previous iteration's deposit!; nothing is recomputed on a read. The later stages compute their recoveries into those same slots, so the step allocates nothing and occupies no storage beyond its own spectral stage registers. The cache's fields epoch — the freshness counter every new deposit is recorded at — is advanced when the step completes.

The two calls of a hand-run loop align: step! leads with the dispatch tag and mutates its second argument, while deposit! leads with cache, the object it mutates; after that first argument, the shared trio state, domain, buffers appears in the same relative order in both.

Returns state.

A step alone leaves the cache mid-iteration

A step! alone leaves the cache mid-iteration (its slots taken as scratch): the caller closes the iteration with deposit!, which re-fills every slot, at the new epoch, from the advanced state — a hand-run loop is step! then deposit!, and the run's advance! does both.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.deposit! — Function
deposit!(cache, state, domain, buffers)
deposit!(cache, tracers, u; traversal = nothing)
deposit!(sim)

Close a producer's advance (the step or the tracer push) by depositing the observables it has just made measurable: compute them from the arguments, write them into cache (an ObservablesCache), and record each slot at the current epoch (the cache's freshness counter). One generic serves both sides of the run because it is one concept — each advance, of the fields or of the tracers, is followed by its own deposit, so an iteration closes as step! then deposit!, then advect! then deposit! again.

Arguments

  • cache — every form's destination: the cache whose slots receive the deposits.
  • state — field form: the ResolvedFields the step has just produced.
  • domain — field form: the FixedDomain whose plans run the recoveries.
  • buffers — field form: the scheme's IntegratorBuffers, one spectral stage register of which — dead at the iteration's end — is the recovery scratch.
  • tracers — particle form: the Tracers whose current positions are sampled, with the set's own interpolation scheme.
  • u — particle form: the velocity field in physical representation to interpolate — normally the field form's own fresh deposit.
  • traversal = nothing — particle form: the visiting order of a ParticleTraversal (its order field) for the gather, or nothing for the tracers' own order; the values are identical either way.
  • sim — sim form: the Simulation to unpack.

The field form deposits the field-derived slots — the velocity, vorticity and, when the run carries one, scalar recoveries in physical representation, then the maximum-speed reduction over the just-deposited velocity — from the state the step has just produced. Every recovery is work the next iteration's first stage would have done anyway, so closing the iteration costs no extra transforms — one extra pass only, the maximum-speed reduction, which runs every iteration even under a hand-fixed $\Delta t$ that never reads it.

The particle form deposits the one trajectory-bound slot: it interpolates u at the tracer set's current positions and stamps the slot against both counters — the fields epoch (its count of state changes) of the interpolated velocity and the particles epoch (of position changes) of the positions, since the velocity at the particles is a function of the two together. It therefore runs after the tracer push, which itself consumed the field form's deposit.

The deposited values serve twice: they are the Lagrangian velocity a caller accumulates, and the start-of-step velocity the next advect! reads — $M \times 3$ values for $M$ tracers, where keeping the endpoint field would cost $N^{3} \times 3$ on a grid of $N$ points per axis.

Every iteration ends with these deposits: the run's advance! performs them for you, the Simulation constructor makes the first deposits so the first iteration finds the initial state's observables, and a hand-run loop calls each form itself after the advance it closes. After mutating the state or the positions from outside the iteration, announce it — fields_modified! / particles_modified! — and re-deposit with the sim form, which performs every deposit the run's configuration carries.

Returns cache (or sim).

Timestep ceiling

This section documents a scheme's advective-CFL ceiling.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Propagator.recommended_cfl — Function
recommended_cfl(scheme, domain)

A conservative advective-CFL (Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy) ceiling $C_{\mathrm{CFL}}$ for the scheme scheme on the FixedDomain domain, for a timestep $\Delta t = C_{\mathrm{CFL}} \Delta x / \max_{\boldsymbol{x}}\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert$, with $\Delta x = L/N$ the grid spacing, $L$ the box side and $N$ the grid points per axis.

The advective eigenvalues of the dealiased system are imaginary, of magnitude up to $\kappa_{\max}^{\mathrm{ret}}\sup\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert$, with $\kappa_{\max}^{\mathrm{ret}}$ the highest wavenumber the dealiasing keeps — a rate. Stability needs that rate times the step, which once $\Delta t$ is substituted is $C_{\mathrm{CFL}} \kappa_{\max}^{\mathrm{ret}}\Delta x$, to stay inside the scheme's imaginary-axis stability limit $y_{\mathrm{s}}$, a pure number.

Two loosenesses sit in that estimate and they pull in opposite directions. The speed in the denominator is max_speed, a maximum over grid points, so the continuum supremum between them can be larger — making the computed step too long. Against it, the eigenvalue bound over-estimates the true spectral radius, since not every mode carries the peak speed — making the ceiling too small. They are independent, so the second is not guaranteed to cover the first, which is why the base ceiling carries a margin.

That base is calibrated for the $2/3$ rule, where $\kappa_{\max}^{\mathrm{ret}}\Delta x = 2\pi/3 \approx 2.09$. For IFLowStorageRK3 the limit $y_{\mathrm{s}} = \sqrt{3}$ gives $C_{\mathrm{CFL}} \lesssim 0.83$, taken conservatively as $0.5$; IFSSPRK3 shares that $0.5$ exactly, every three-stage third-order explicit scheme having the same stability function; IFLowStorageRK4 scales it by the ratio of imaginary-axis extents, $y_{\mathrm{s}} \approx 3.34$ against $\sqrt{3}$, rounded down to $0.96$. IFHeun is imaginary-axis unstable ($y_{\mathrm{s}} = 0$, relying on viscous damping), which makes the formula degenerate: its $0.25$ is an empirical margin rather than a computed limit.

The base is then scaled by the domain's dealiasing: a strategy that keeps modes to a higher $\kappa_{\max}^{\mathrm{ret}}$ (e.g. HouLi, out to the Nyquist wavenumber) takes a proportionally smaller step, $C_{\mathrm{CFL}} \propto 1/\kappa_{\max}^{\mathrm{ret}}$. That scaling applies to every scheme's base, IFHeun's empirical one included: for a computed base it is the geometry of the retained band; for the empirical base it is an extrapolation in the same proportion, not a derived limit — and the scaled value is only as trustworthy as its base. This is the value Simulation uses; override cfl for a specific run.

The IFHeun margin does not know the viscosity

This function takes no $\nu$, by design — a CFL ceiling is a property of the scheme and the grid — but IFHeun's margin is the one value that depends on a quantity it cannot see. The same 0.25 is returned at every Reynolds number while the viscous damping it rests on weakens as $\nu$ falls. No code-level guard exists — adding one would give a grid-and-scheme property a physics argument. The practical course at high Reynolds number is to prefer IFLowStorageRK3, whose ceiling has no such dependence, or to pass an explicit cfl and verify stability directly: the run's energy must stay bounded over a trial stretch.
