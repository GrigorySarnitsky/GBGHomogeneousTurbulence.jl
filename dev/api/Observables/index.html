Observables

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables — Module
Observables

The measured functions of the flow state:

  • kinetic_energy, scalar_variance — the turbulent kinetic energy and the scalar variance.
  • dissipation, scalar_dissipation — their dissipation rates.
  • derivative_skewness — the velocity-derivative skewness, the K41 (Kolmogorov 1941) marker of developed turbulence.
  • max_speed — the maximum velocity component, which sets the advective-CFL step.
  • energy_spectrum, integral_scale — the measured energy spectrum and the integral length scale derived from it.

Their plateaus gate warm-up (warmup!). Each reduces a field to a scalar (the energy spectrum, to a distribution over wavenumber); none dealiases first. The quadratic reductions (kinetic_energy, dissipation, scalar_variance, scalar_dissipation) are Parseval-exact: each is a normalized inner product, summed exactly from the spectral coefficients regardless of representation; the equivalent physical-space mean picks up no aliasing either, because the quadratic's zero mode folds in nothing when $2\kappa_{\max} < N$ (the $2/3$ rule). The cubic derivative_skewness is not: its grid mean folds a small residual from the truncation edge back into the mean, so it carries a slight aliasing on a $2/3$ field (negligible when well-resolved; exact cubic dealiasing would need the $1/2$ rule).

Energy and variance

The velocity kinetic energy and its scalar analogue, the variance — both Parseval quadratic invariants.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.kinetic_energy — Function
kinetic_energy(u)

The turbulent kinetic energy $\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert^{2}\rangle$ of the velocity field u. Computed as $\tfrac{1}{2} \langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts u in either representation.

source
GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.scalar_variance — Function
scalar_variance(φ)

The scalar variance $\langle\varphi^{2}\rangle$ of the scalar field φ (for a mean-zero $\varphi$). Computed as $\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts φ in either representation.

Dissipation rates

The kinetic-energy dissipation ε and the scalar-variance dissipation ε_φ.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.dissipation — Function
dissipation(ω, viscosity)
dissipation(û, domain, viscosity)

The mean kinetic-energy dissipation rate of a homogeneous flow — a single number, not a field — at the kinematic viscosity $\nu$:

\[\varepsilon = \nu \langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle = 2\nu\langle S_{ij}S_{ij}\rangle,\]

the two forms equal for a homogeneous flow, with the strain-rate tensor $S_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x_j}u_i + \partial_{x_i}u_j)$.

Two routes to the same rate; take the one whose input is already in hand.

  • dissipation(ω, viscosity) — from a vorticity field ω $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ in either representation, as the normalized inner product (Parseval). Use it when $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ exists for its own sake: the cached velocity, or a vorticity the caller supplies.
  • dissipation(û, domain, viscosity) — straight from the velocity in spectral representation û $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$, with the FixedDomain domain supplying the multiplier, and no vorticity formed at all. Use it to measure off the state, where a $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ would be built only to be reduced away.

The second route trades the curl for a $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}$-weighted spectral inner product against the domain.squared_wavenumber_kernel multiplier: from

\[\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\times\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2} = \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2} - \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2},\]

the enstrophy of a divergence-free velocity is its Dirichlet ($H^{1}$) energy, $\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle = \langle\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rangle$.

Warning

The û route drops the $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2}$ term, so it holds only for a divergence-free velocity — exact for a state advanced by the Leray-projected dynamics, wrong for an arbitrary vector field. The vorticity route carries no such condition. (The scalar twin, scalar_dissipation, is unconditional: a scalar has no solenoidal constraint to invoke.)

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.scalar_dissipation — Function
scalar_dissipation(φ̂, domain, diffusivity)

The mean scalar-variance dissipation rate $\varepsilon_\varphi = 2\varkappa \langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$, from the scalar field in spectral representation φ̂ $\hat\varphi$, the FixedDomain bundle domain, and the scalar diffusivity $\varkappa = \nu/\mathrm{Sc}$. The gradient energy $\langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$ is the Dirichlet ($H^{1}$) energy $\langle\varphi,\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\varphi\rangle$ — a $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}$-weighted spectral inner product against the domain.squared_wavenumber_kernel multiplier, formed straight from the spectral coefficients with no physical gradient $\nabla\varphi$ materialized. It is the scalar twin of dissipation's spectral route, and unconditional where that one needs a divergence-free velocity.

Derivative skewness

The velocity-derivative skewness — the developed-turbulence marker that gates warm-up.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.derivative_skewness — Function
derivative_skewness(û, domain)

The velocity-derivative skewness $S$, written coordinate-free as the normalized third invariant of the strain-rate tensor $\boldsymbol{S} = \operatorname{sym}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}$ (the italic $S$ is the scalar skewness, the bold $\boldsymbol{S}$ the strain-rate tensor):

\[\begin{aligned} S &= C \frac{\langle\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle^{3/2}} = 3C \frac{\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle^{3/2}}, \\ C &= \tfrac{4}{7}\sqrt{15/2} \approx 1.5649. \end{aligned}\]

Because $\boldsymbol{S}$ is symmetric and, for the incompressible field, traceless, $\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3} = 3\det\boldsymbol{S}$. By isotropy this equals the classical longitudinal skewness $\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{3}\rangle / \langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{2}\rangle^{3/2}$; the constant follows from $\langle\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3}\rangle = \tfrac{105}{8}\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{3}\rangle$ and $\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle = \tfrac{15}{2}\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{2}\rangle$ (Betchov 1956). So the reported value is the familiar $S \approx -0.5$ of developed turbulence, but from a genuine tensor invariant rather than an axis-dependent gradient.

From the velocity field û in spectral representation, the strain is formed directly with symmetric_gradient and the wavenumber multipliers of the FixedDomain bundle domain (skipping the full nine-component velocity gradient), carried to physical space by its rank-2 tensor transform plans, and reduced to $\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ (inner) and $\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ (det); domain also sizes the per-call tensor and scalar scratch. Because $\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ is a cubic invariant, its grid mean carries a small residual aliasing on a $2/3$-truncated field — negligible for a well-resolved run, but, unlike the quadratic reductions, not exactly alias-free. $S$ is the classic marker of developed turbulence: a random-phase (Gaussian) field has $S \approx 0$ (all odd invariants vanish), while fully developed isotropic turbulence has $S \approx -0.5$ (an empirical, weakly Reynolds-number-dependent value) — the enstrophy-production / vortex-stretching signature. Its plateau is the strict "real turbulence yet" gate of the warm-up detector.

Timestep control

The maximum velocity component, which sets the advective-CFL step — a max-norm reduction, not a physics invariant.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.max_speed — Function
max_speed(u)

The largest velocity component in absolute value, $\max_i\lvert u_i\rvert$, of the velocity field u in physical representation — an allocation-free reduction used to set the advective-CFL timestep. It differs from the true $\sup\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert$ by at most $\sqrt{3}$, a gap absorbed by the conservative recommended_cfl ceiling (whose eigenvalue bound over-estimates the true spectral radius).

Spectrum and length scale

The measured energy spectrum and the integral scale — the measured counterparts of a Phenomenology model spectrum, for validation.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.energy_spectrum — Function
energy_spectrum(û, domain)

The measured kinetic-energy spectrum $E(\kappa)$ of the velocity field û (in spectral representation) — the turbulent kinetic energy resolved over wavenumber-magnitude shells, $k = \sum_\kappa E(\kappa)$. Returns (κ, E): the shell-center wavenumber magnitudes and the energy in each shell (brought to the host, an occasional diagnostic). It is the GBGPeriodicFields radial spectrum of û halved — the field reduction sums $\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^2$ while the kinetic energy carries the $\tfrac{1}{2}$. The domain (a FixedDomain) supplies the wavenumber multipliers. This is the measured counterpart of a Phenomenology model spectrum, for validation.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.integral_scale — Function
integral_scale(û, domain)

The longitudinal integral length scale of the velocity field û, measured from its energy_spectrum $E(\kappa)$,

\[L_{11} = \frac{3\pi}{4} \frac{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)/\kappa\,\mathrm d\kappa}{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)\,\mathrm d\kappa}.\]

$L_{11}$ is the large-eddy scale — the one flow parameter a Phenomenology model spectrum needs that the small scales do not fix. The integrals above are the idealized continuous definition; they are evaluated as sums over the measured energy_spectrum shells (whose per-shell energy makes the shell width cancel in the ratio). The domain (a FixedDomain) supplies the wavenumber multipliers.

For reference, two related scales follow from the same spectrum. The transverse integral scale is $L_{22} = L_{11}/2$ (the incompressible-isotropic relation). The scale defined from the full trace velocity correlation $R_{ii}(r) = \langle\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x}+r\boldsymbol{e})\rangle$ ($\boldsymbol{e}$ a unit vector) — with no directional projection — is

\[\begin{aligned} L_E &= \frac{\pi}{2} \frac{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)/\kappa\,\mathrm d\kappa}{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)\,\mathrm d\kappa} \\ &= \tfrac{2}{3}L_{11} = \tfrac{1}{3}(L_{11} + 2L_{22}), \end{aligned}\]

the isotropic average of the one longitudinal and two transverse contributions.
