ParticlePropagator

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.ParticlePropagator — Module
ParticlePropagator

It advances Lagrangian particles in time:

  • AbstractParticleIntegrator — the particle integration schemes: Heun, ABM and AB.
  • advect! — advance the trajectories one step under the chosen scheme.
  • deposit! — this module adds the particle method: interpolate the flow velocity at the tracers' new positions and add it, with its time, to the trail the observables cache keeps. One value has two consumers: the Lagrangian statistics a caller accumulates, and the next advance, which integrates over it.

The particles themselves, and what is measured at them, are in Particles.

The particle integration scheme

How the positions are advanced over one step — the Lagrangian counterpart of the field schemes. AB integrates over the Lagrangian samples already deposited, at one interpolation per step; ABM corrects that step with a second interpolation, and Heun is its one-step member. A scheme owns no storage: it works on the positions and on the samples the observables cache keeps.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.ParticlePropagator.AbstractParticleIntegrator — Type
AbstractParticleIntegrator

The supertype of the particle integration schemes — how the tracer positions are advanced over one step, the Lagrangian counterpart of the field side's AbstractIntegrator. A scheme is a value (Heun(), ABM(4)), given to a run as the particle_integration_scheme of its ParticleTracking and passed to advect!.

The family has two branches: AB, which reuses the Lagrangian samples the run already deposits and interpolates once per step, and ABM, which interpolates a second time to correct the step, with Heun its one-step member. Which trade a run wants is for its user to measure — one interpolation per step against a smaller error at equal order.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.ParticlePropagator.Heun — Type
Heun()

Second-order Heun-in-time on the velocity at the two ends of the step — the particle integration scheme a run uses by default, and the one-step member ABM(1) of the ABM family. It advances the positions with the start-of-step Lagrangian velocity the previous iteration's closing particle deposit! left in the cache and the end-of-step velocity field the solver has just produced, so it needs nothing from inside the field scheme's stages.

It keeps two samples of the velocity at the particles, the current one and the one before. One step of advect! over $\Delta t$ is

\[\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{v}_1 &= \boldsymbol{u}(t_n, \boldsymbol{X}), & \boldsymbol{X}^{*} &= \boldsymbol{X} + \Delta t \boldsymbol{v}_1, \\ \boldsymbol{v}_2 &= \boldsymbol{u}(t_{n+1}, \boldsymbol{X}^{*}), & \boldsymbol{X} &\leftarrow \boldsymbol{X} + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}(\boldsymbol{v}_1 + \boldsymbol{v}_2), \end{aligned}\]

with positions wrapped into $[0, L)$, $L$ the box side.

Pair it with the default interpolant unless the grid is coarse

Under the advective-CFL loop $\Delta t \propto \Delta x$, so this scheme's error falls as $\Delta x^{2}$ while a degree-$d$ interpolant's trajectory floor falls as $\Delta x^{d+1}$. For the default CatmullRom (degree 2) the floor therefore falls faster than the time error, and the two cross over: measured on a band-limited field of $N$ grid points per axis, interpolation dominates at $N = 16{-}32$ and the time error dominates from $N \approx 64$ on (at $N = 128$ the floor is $1\times10^{-5}$ against a time error of $6\times10^{-5}$; the numbers are setup-specific, and only the $\Delta x^{d+1}$ trend generalizes).

So at production resolutions CatmullRom is not what limits a Heun trajectory and a wider stencil buys nothing; at coarse resolution, or with $\Delta t$ cut well below the CFL value, LalescuSpline(8; smoothness = 1) — same $C^1$, degree 3 — moves the floor down by more than an order of magnitude. What governs this in a run is how well the grid resolves the Kolmogorov length $\eta$, so read $N$ here as a stand-in for $\kappa_{\max}\eta$, with $\kappa_{\max}$ the largest resolved wavenumber.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.ParticlePropagator.ABM — Type
ABM(k)

The $k$-step Adams–Bashforth–Moulton predictor–corrector for the tracer positions, of order $k + 1$: an AB step over $k$ samples predicts the positions, the velocity is interpolated there, and the $k$-step Adams–Moulton formula corrects the step with it. The argument k counts the samples the predictor integrates over, from 1 to 7, so the order is one more than the number in the name, and ABM(1) is Heun. The limit keeps the order at 8 or below, beyond which the quadrature that computes the weights is no longer exact.

Writing $\boldsymbol{f}_{n-j} = \boldsymbol{u}(t_{n-j}, \boldsymbol{X}(t_{n-j}))$ for the deposited samples of the velocity at the particles, one step of advect! is

\[\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{X}^{*} &= \boldsymbol{X} + \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} b_j \boldsymbol{f}_{n-j}, & \boldsymbol{f}^{*} &= \boldsymbol{u}(t_{n+1}, \boldsymbol{X}^{*}), \\ \boldsymbol{X} &\leftarrow \boldsymbol{X} + \beta^{*} \boldsymbol{f}^{*} + \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \beta_j \boldsymbol{f}_{n-j}, \end{aligned}\]

with positions wrapped into the box. The weights $b_j$ integrate, over the step, the polynomial through the $k$ samples, and $\beta^{*}$, $\beta_j$ the polynomial through those and $\boldsymbol{f}^{*}$ (Hairer, Nørsett & Wanner 1993). They are recomputed every step from the samples' actual times, because the run's timestep changes from one iteration to the next.

Against AB(k + 1), which has the same order and keeps the same number of samples, this scheme interpolates twice per step and makes a smaller error.

The run's trail of the velocity at the particles (velocity_at_particles_trail) keeps $k + 1$ samples under this scheme. The scheme has no samples to work from at the start, so the first $k - 1$ steps are taken with ABM(1), …, ABM(k - 1). Those lower-order steps leave an error of third order behind, and every step between the trail's samples is a full one after $2k - 1$ iterations: a measurement that needs the full accuracy either discards those iterations or sets shortened_first_steps on ParticleTracking.

References

  • Hairer, E., Nørsett, S. P. & Wanner, G. (1993). Solving Ordinary Differential Equations I: Nonstiff Problems. 2nd ed., Springer.

The scheme stores no fields: k is carried as a type parameter.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.ParticlePropagator.AB — Type
AB(k)

Variable-step Adams–Bashforth trajectory integration of order $k$ — the scheme TurTLE uses for its tracers, at $k = 4$ (Lalescu et al. 2022) — buying accuracy in time at no extra field evaluation and no extra interpolation per step. The argument k is the number of samples used, from 1 to 8, the order beyond which the quadrature that computes the weights is no longer exact. AB(1) is forward Euler, which is first order and should not be used for a run.

Where ABM interpolates a second time, at predicted positions, Adams–Bashforth reuses the Lagrangian samples the run has already deposited at earlier instants and stops there. One step of advect! is

\[\boldsymbol{X} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{X} + \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} b_j \boldsymbol{u}(t_{n-j}, \boldsymbol{X}(t_{n-j})),\]

with the weights $b_j$ re-solved each step by integrating, over the step, the polynomial through the samples at their actual times (Hairer, Nørsett & Wanner 1993). The steps are not assumed uniform: the run's timestep changes every iteration, which classical fixed-step coefficients would silently get wrong.

The run's trail of the velocity at the particles (velocity_at_particles_trail) keeps $k$ samples under this scheme. The scheme has no samples to work from at the start, so the first $k - 1$ steps are taken with ABM(1), …, ABM(k - 1). Those lower-order steps leave an error of third order behind, and every step between the trail's samples is a full one after $2k - 2$ iterations: a measurement that needs the full accuracy either discards those iterations or sets shortened_first_steps on ParticleTracking.

The interval of stability shrinks fast with $k$. A displacement of the trajectory in a direction the flow compresses at the rate $\lambda$ is damped only while $\lambda\Delta t$ stays below $0.30$ for AB(4), $0.088$ for AB(6) and $0.024$ for AB(8), and a region of intense strain can exceed the last two at an ordinary timestep. The corrected scheme of the same order, ABM(k - 1), keeps $1.9$, $1.0$ and $0.58$: above $k = 4$ it is the one to use.

The interpolant sets a floor no timestep can cross

Interpolating the velocity perturbs the field the tracers follow, and that perturbation does not shrink with $\Delta t$: for a perturbation of size $\delta$ and a flow with Lipschitz constant $\Lambda$ over a span $t$, Grönwall bounds the trajectory error by $\delta(\mathrm{e}^{\Lambda t} - 1)/\Lambda$ however the time integration is done. Refining the step, or raising k, pays only until the time error reaches that floor.

The floor is set by the interpolant's polynomial reproduction degree, not its smoothness class — measured here, raising the degree at fixed $C^1$ lowered it fifty-fold, while varying the smoothness at fixed degree left the time convergence unchanged. Raise the degree when the floor is what limits you; smoothness buys something different, namely readouts that differentiate the interpolant, such as acceleration (Lalescu, Teaca & Carati 2010).

References

  • Hairer, E., Nørsett, S. P. & Wanner, G. (1993). Solving Ordinary Differential Equations I: Nonstiff Problems. 2nd ed., Springer.
  • Lalescu, C. C., Bramas, B., Rampp, M. & Wilczek, M. (2022). An efficient particle tracking algorithm for large-scale parallel pseudo-spectral simulations of turbulence. Comput. Phys. Commun. 278, 108406.
  • Lalescu, C. C., Teaca, B. & Carati, D. (2010). Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields. J. Comput. Phys. 229(17), 5862–5869.

The particle step

Advance the tracers once the fields have moved. The step is closed by the particle form of deposit!, which records the flow velocity where each tracer now sits: the next step integrates over it.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.ParticlePropagator.advect! — Function
advect!(scheme, tracers, domain, cache, dt; traversal = nothing) -> tracers

Advance the positions of a tracer set by one step, in place, under the particle integration scheme scheme, whose docstring gives the update.

Arguments

  • scheme — the particle integration scheme, an AbstractParticleIntegrator.
  • tracers — the Tracers whose positions advance.
  • domain — the FixedDomain, which supplies the box side the positions are wrapped into.
  • cache — the run's ObservablesCache. The step reads two things from it: the velocity field at the end of the step, which the field deposit has just left there, and the trail of the velocity at the particles, whose samples it integrates over.
  • dt — the step size $\Delta t$, positive.
  • traversal = nothing — a ParticleTraversal, to gather the velocity in its memory-locality order; nothing gathers in tracer order, with identical values.

The step refuses to run, with an ErrorException, unless the fields have taken exactly one step past the tracer positions and the trail's newest sample was taken at those positions: otherwise it would move the tracers with a velocity from the wrong time. A dt that is not positive and finite, and a cache whose trail does not keep the number of samples scheme works from, are each an ArgumentError. The step also advances the cache's particles epoch, the version of the positions, so a read of the trail throws as stale until the particle deposit! closing the iteration adds the sample at the new positions.

While the trail holds fewer samples than the scheme integrates over, the step is taken with the lower member of the ABM family that the samples present allow.
