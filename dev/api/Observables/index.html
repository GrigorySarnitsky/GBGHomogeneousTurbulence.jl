Observables

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables — Module
Observables

The functions of the flow state that are measured — kinetic energy, dissipation, the velocity- derivative skewness, the scalar variance and its dissipation, and the maximum component speed. In the Mori–Zwanzig reading (design doc §8) these are the observables of the Dynamics: their plateaus gate warm-up (warmup!), and their statistics are tracked by GBGStreamingStatistics. Each reduces a field to a scalar.

Public API

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.derivative_skewness — Method
derivative_skewness(uh, ops, ws)

The velocity-derivative skewness

\[S = \frac{\big\langle(\partial_{x_i} u_i)^3\big\rangle}{\big\langle(\partial_{x_i} u_i)^2\big\rangle^{3/2}},\]

averaged over the three diagonal components $i = 1,2,3$ for isotropy, of the velocity uh (given in spectral representation). Each diagonal derivative $\partial_{x_i} u_i$ is formed spectrally (gradient!), transformed to physical space, and reduced to its second and third moments.

$S$ is the classic marker of developed turbulence: a random-phase (Gaussian) field has $S \approx 0$ (a linear image of a Gaussian is Gaussian, hence symmetric), while fully developed isotropic turbulence has $S \approx -0.5$ (Kolmogorov). Its plateau is the strict "real turbulence yet" gate of the warm-up detector. No field-sized allocation — reuses the workspace scratch (ws.scratch, ws.velocity_star).

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.dissipation — Method
dissipation(vorticity, viscosity)

The mean kinetic-energy dissipation rate of a homogeneous flow, $\varepsilon = \nu\,\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle$, from the physical vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and the kinematic viscosity $\nu$ (the homogeneous identity $\varepsilon = 2\nu\langle S_{ij}S_{ij}\rangle = \nu\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle$).

source
GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.kinetic_energy — Method
kinetic_energy(velocity)

The turbulent kinetic energy $\tfrac12\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert^{2}\rangle$ of the velocity field velocity. Computed as $\tfrac12\,\langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts velocity in either representation.

source
GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.max_speed — Method
max_speed(velocity)

The largest velocity component in absolute value, $\max_i\lvert u_i\rvert$ — an allocation-free reduction used to set the advective CFL timestep. It differs from the true $\sup\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert$ by at most $\sqrt{3}$, well inside the CFL margin.

source
GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.scalar_dissipation — Method
scalar_dissipation(scalar_gradient, diffusivity)

The mean scalar-variance dissipation rate $\varepsilon_\varphi = 2\varkappa\,\langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$, from the physical scalar gradient scalar_gradient $\nabla\varphi$ and the scalar diffusivity $\varkappa = \nu/\mathrm{Sc}$.

source
GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.scalar_variance — Method
scalar_variance(scalar)

The scalar variance $\langle\varphi^{2}\rangle$ of the scalar field scalar (for a mean-zero $\varphi$). Computed as $\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts scalar in either representation.
