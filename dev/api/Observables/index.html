Observables

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables — Module
Observables

The functions of the flow state that are measured — kinetic energy, dissipation, the velocity- derivative skewness, the scalar variance and its dissipation, and the maximum component speed. In the Mori–Zwanzig reading (design doc §8) these are the observables of the Dynamics: their plateaus gate warm-up (warmup!), and their statistics are to be tracked by GBGStreamingStatistics. Each reduces a field to a scalar.

source

Energy and variance

The velocity kinetic energy and its scalar analogue, the variance — both Parseval quadratic invariants.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.kinetic_energy — Function
kinetic_energy(u)

The turbulent kinetic energy $\tfrac12\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert^{2}\rangle$ of the velocity field u. Computed as $\tfrac12\,\langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts u in either representation.

source
GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.scalar_variance — Function
scalar_variance(φ)

The scalar variance $\langle\varphi^{2}\rangle$ of the scalar field φ (for a mean-zero $\varphi$). Computed as $\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts φ in either representation.

Dissipation rates

The kinetic-energy dissipation ε and the scalar-variance dissipation ε_φ.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.dissipation — Function
dissipation(ω, viscosity)

The mean kinetic-energy dissipation rate of a homogeneous flow, $\varepsilon = \nu\,\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle$, from the physical ω $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and the kinematic viscosity $\nu$ (the homogeneous identity $\varepsilon = 2\nu\langle S_{ij}S_{ij}\rangle = \nu\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle$, with the strain-rate tensor $S_{ij} = \tfrac12(\partial_{x_j}u_i + \partial_{x_i}u_j)$).

source
GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.scalar_dissipation — Function
scalar_dissipation(φ, ops, diffusivity)

The mean scalar-variance dissipation rate $\varepsilon_\varphi = 2\varkappa\,\langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$, from the scalar field φ $\varphi$ (in spectral representation), the SpectralOperators bundle ops, and the scalar diffusivity $\varkappa = \nu/\mathrm{Sc}$. The gradient energy $\langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$ is the Dirichlet ($H^{1}$) energy $\langle\varphi,\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\varphi\rangle$, formed by a fused weighted_inner against the squared-wavenumber multiplier ops.squared_wavenumber_kernel — straight from the spectral coefficients, with no physical gradient $\nabla\varphi$ materialized (unlike the velocity dissipation's cached $\boldsymbol{\omega}$, $\nabla\varphi$ is not otherwise formed by the scalar right-hand side).

Derivative skewness

The velocity-derivative skewness — the developed-turbulence marker that gates warm-up.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.derivative_skewness — Function
derivative_skewness(û, ops)

The velocity-derivative skewness $S$, written coordinate-free as the normalized third invariant of the strain-rate tensor $\boldsymbol{S} = \operatorname{sym}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}$ (the italic $S$ is the scalar skewness, the bold $\boldsymbol{S}$ the strain-rate tensor):

\[S = C\,\frac{\langle\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle^{3/2}} = 3C\,\frac{\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle^{3/2}}, \qquad C = \tfrac{4}{7}\sqrt{\tfrac{15}{2}} \approx 1.5649.\]

Because $\boldsymbol{S}$ is symmetric and, for the incompressible field, traceless, $\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3} = 3\det\boldsymbol{S}$. By isotropy this equals the classical longitudinal skewness $\langle(\partial u_1/\partial x_1)^{3}\rangle / \langle(\partial u_1/\partial x_1)^{2}\rangle^{3/2}$; the constant follows from $\langle\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3}\rangle = \tfrac{105}{8}\langle(\partial u_1/\partial x_1)^{3}\rangle$ and $\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle = \tfrac{15}{2}\langle(\partial u_1/\partial x_1)^{2}\rangle$ (Betchov). So the reported value is the familiar $S \approx -0.5$ of developed turbulence, but from a genuine tensor invariant rather than an axis-dependent gradient.

From the velocity field û in spectral representation, the strain is formed directly with symmetric_gradient and the wavenumber multipliers of the SpectralOperators bundle ops (skipping the full nine-component velocity gradient), carried to physical space by its rank-2 tensor transform plans, and reduced to $\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ (inner) and $\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ (det); ops also sizes the per-call tensor and scalar scratch. $S$ is the classic marker of developed turbulence: a random-phase (Gaussian) field has $S \approx 0$ (all odd invariants vanish), while fully developed isotropic turbulence has $S \approx -0.5$ (Kolmogorov) — the enstrophy-production / vortex-stretching signature. Its plateau is the strict "real turbulence yet" gate of the warm-up detector.

Timestep control

The maximum velocity component, which sets the advective-CFL step — a max-norm reduction, not a physics invariant.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.max_speed — Function
max_speed(u)

The largest velocity component in absolute value, $\max_i\lvert u_i\rvert$ — an allocation-free reduction used to set the advective CFL timestep. It differs from the true $\sup\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert$ by at most $\sqrt{3}$, well inside the CFL margin.
