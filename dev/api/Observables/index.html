Observables

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables — Module
Observables

The measured functions of the flow state:

  • kinetic_energy, scalar_variance — the turbulent kinetic energy and the scalar variance.
  • dissipation, scalar_dissipation — their dissipation rates.
  • derivative_skewness — the velocity-derivative skewness, the classical marker of a developed cascade.
  • max_speed — the fastest flow speed on the grid, which sets the advective-CFL step (one generic shared with Cache: the field reduction is homed here, the cached read there).
  • vorticity_physical! — the vorticity recovered in physical representation from the spectral state, the one field-valued observable (its cached read, vorticity_physical, lives in Cache).
  • energy_spectrum, integral_scale — the measured energy spectrum and the integral length scale derived from it.

Each returns the field's own real element type rather than a fixed Float64, so a Float32 run measures in Float32. Each is a function of the state: most reduce a field to a scalar, the energy spectrum reduces to a distribution over wavenumber, and the vorticity recovery alone produces a field. Three of them gate the automatic warm-up (warmup!), which advances until the dissipation $\varepsilon$ stops drifting, the derivative skewness reaches its plateau, and — when the scalar is forced — the scalar variance settles too. The kinetic energy is deliberately not among them: a constant-energy forcing clamps it flat and a fixed-injection forcing makes it ring, so it is measured (it normalizes the eddy-turnover clock) but never gated on. None dealiases first — the quadratic reductions are exactly alias-free under a truncating dealiasing band (see kinetic_energy for the condition; HouLi meets it only to machine precision), and the cubic skewness carries the slight residual its own docstring prices.

Energy and variance

The velocity kinetic energy and its scalar analogue, the variance — both Parseval quadratic invariants.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.kinetic_energy — Function
kinetic_energy(u)
kinetic_energy(û)

The turbulent kinetic energy $\texttt{kinetic\_energy} \equiv k = \tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert^{2}\rangle$ of the velocity field $\boldsymbol{u}$ (the u argument). Computed as $\tfrac{1}{2} \langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts the velocity in either representation — the physical field $\boldsymbol{u}$ or its spectral coefficients $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$, to the same value. Like every quadratic reduction here (dissipation, scalar_variance, scalar_dissipation), it needs no dealiasing of its own: the sum over spectral coefficients is exact, and the equivalent physical-space mean picks up no aliasing either, because a quadratic's zero mode folds in nothing when $2\kappa_{\max} < N$ in units of the fundamental $2\pi/L$ — satisfied by the $2/3$ rule, by PhaseShift's wider $\sqrt{2}N/3$ band, and by every narrower band. HouLi keeps the full band $\kappa_{\max} = N/2$, where the only pairs that fold to zero are Nyquist modes its taper has already damped to machine precision — alias-free in practice, not exactly.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.scalar_variance — Function
scalar_variance(φ)
scalar_variance(φ̂)

The scalar variance $\langle\varphi^{2}\rangle$ of the scalar field φ (for a mean-zero $\varphi$). Computed as $\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts φ in either representation.

Dissipation rates

The kinetic-energy dissipation $\varepsilon$ and the scalar-variance dissipation $\varepsilon_\varphi$.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.dissipation — Function
dissipation(ω, viscosity)
dissipation(û, domain, viscosity)

The mean kinetic-energy dissipation rate of a homogeneous flow — a single number, not a field — at the kinematic viscosity $\nu$ (the viscosity argument):

\[\begin{aligned} \varepsilon &= \nu \langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle \\ &= 2\nu\langle S_{ij}S_{ij}\rangle, \end{aligned}\]

the two forms equal for a homogeneous, divergence-free flow (periodicity alone removes the boundary terms; the remaining difference is the dilatation $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}$, zero for the Leray-projected state a run advances), with the strain-rate tensor $S_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x_j}u_i + \partial_{x_i}u_j)$.

Two routes reach the same rate; take the one whose input is already in hand. The first form measures from a vorticity field $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ (the ω argument) in either representation, as the normalized inner product (Parseval) — use it where $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ exists for its own sake, as the cached vorticity does. The second measures straight from the velocity in spectral representation $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ (the û argument), with the FixedDomain domain supplying the multiplier and no vorticity formed at all — use it to measure off the state, where a $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ would be built only to be reduced away.

The second route trades the curl for a $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}$-weighted spectral inner product against the domain.squared_wavenumber_kernel multiplier: from

\[\begin{aligned} \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\times\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2} &= \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2} - \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2}, \end{aligned}\]

the mean-square vorticity of a divergence-free velocity is its Dirichlet energy — the mean square of its gradient, $H^1$ in the Sobolev naming — $\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle = \langle\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rangle$.

The velocity route holds only for a divergence-free field

The û route drops the $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2}$ term, so it holds only for a divergence-free velocity — exact for a state advanced by the Leray-projected dynamics, wrong for an arbitrary vector field. The vorticity route carries no such condition. (The scalar twin, scalar_dissipation, is unconditional: a scalar has no solenoidal constraint to invoke.)

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.scalar_dissipation — Function
scalar_dissipation(φ̂, domain, diffusivity)

The mean scalar-variance dissipation rate $\varepsilon_\varphi = 2\varkappa \langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$, from the scalar field in spectral representation $\hat\varphi$ (the φ̂ argument), the FixedDomain bundle domain, and the scalar diffusivity $\varkappa = \nu/\mathrm{Sc}$ (the diffusivity argument). The gradient energy $\langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$ — the mean square of the scalar's gradient, the Dirichlet or $H^{1}$ energy in the Sobolev naming — equals $\langle\hat\varphi,\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\hat\varphi\rangle$: a $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}$-weighted spectral inner product against the domain.squared_wavenumber_kernel multiplier, formed straight from the spectral coefficients with no physical gradient $\nabla\varphi$ materialized. It is the scalar twin of dissipation's spectral route, and unconditional where that one needs a divergence-free velocity. There is only this one route: dissipation's second form exists because the vorticity is a cached field a run already holds, while $\nabla\varphi$ is never recovered or cached, so a field-argument twin would have nothing to consume.

Derivative skewness

The velocity-derivative skewness — the developed-turbulence marker that gates warm-up.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.derivative_skewness — Function
derivative_skewness(û, domain)

The velocity-derivative skewness $S$, written coordinate-free as the normalized third invariant of the strain-rate tensor $\boldsymbol{S} = \operatorname{sym}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}$ (the italic $S$ is the scalar skewness, the bold $\boldsymbol{S}$ the strain-rate tensor):

\[\begin{aligned} S &= C_S \frac{\langle\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle^{3/2}} = 3C_S \frac{\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle^{3/2}}, \\ C_S &= \tfrac{4}{7}\sqrt{15/2} \approx 1.5649. \end{aligned}\]

Because $\boldsymbol{S}$ is symmetric and, for the incompressible field, traceless, $\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3} = 3\det\boldsymbol{S}$. By isotropy this equals the classical longitudinal skewness $\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{3}\rangle / \langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{2}\rangle^{3/2}$; the constant follows from $\langle\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3}\rangle = \tfrac{105}{8}\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{3}\rangle$ and $\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle = \tfrac{15}{2}\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{2}\rangle$ (Betchov 1956 — An inequality concerning the production of vorticity in isotropic turbulence, J. Fluid Mech. 1(5), 497–504, doi:10.1017/S0022112056000317). So the reported value is the familiar $S \approx -0.5$ of developed turbulence, but from a genuine tensor invariant rather than an axis-dependent gradient.

From the velocity field û in spectral representation, the strain is formed directly with symmetric_gradient and the wavenumber multipliers of the FixedDomain bundle domain (skipping the full nine-component velocity gradient), carried to physical space by its rank-2 tensor transform plans, and reduced to $\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ (inner) and $\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ (the fused mapped mean over the pointwise determinant, which materializes no determinant field); domain also sizes the per-call tensor scratch. Because $\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ is a cubic invariant, its grid mean carries a small residual aliasing on a $2/3$-truncated field — negligible for a well-resolved run, but, unlike the quadratic reductions, not exactly alias-free. $S$ is the classic marker of developed turbulence: a random-phase (Gaussian) field has $S \approx 0$ (all odd invariants vanish), while fully developed isotropic turbulence has $S \approx -0.5$ (an empirical, weakly Reynolds-number-dependent value) — the enstrophy-production / vortex-stretching signature. Its plateau is the strict "real turbulence yet" gate of the warm-up detector.

Timestep control

The fastest flow speed on the grid, which sets the advective-CFL step — a reduction, not a physics invariant.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.max_speed — Function
max_speed(u)
max_speed(cache)

The fastest flow speed on the grid, $\max_{\boldsymbol{x}}\lvert\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x})\rvert$, of the velocity field u in physical representation — the speed that sets the advective-CFL timestep. The magnitude is formed one grid point at a time and consumed by the maximum immediately, so no magnitude field is materialized.

The second form reads the number back from an ObservablesCache, where the closing deposit left it — the same quantity, measured once per iteration and served without a reduction (or a device readback); it throws an ErrorException when no deposit is current, like every cached read.

A grid maximum, not a continuum supremum

The reduction runs over grid points, so between them the flow can be faster than any sampled value, which makes the sampled maximum an under-estimate — one of the two loosenesses recommended_cfl prices when it sets its margin. A CFL condition bounds the stability of the explicit advective term; it is not by itself a guarantee of accuracy.

Why this observable alone has a cached form

The cache holds exactly what the iteration already computes and leaves alive at its end; its contents follow from the run's fixed computation schedule rather than from anything a caller declares. The maximum speed qualifies because the run itself needs it: on the adaptive-CFL path the next timestep is chosen from it, so the reduction happens whether or not anyone reads the value. Under a hand-fixed $\Delta t$ nothing consumes it and the pass is spent anyway — the schedule is fixed when the cache is built and does not vary with how the run is driven. kinetic_energy and dissipation have no cached form because no part of the iteration leaves one alive at its end; caching either would mean adding a reduction the run does not otherwise perform, which is the one thing the cache may not do.

Vorticity recovery

The vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \nabla\times\boldsymbol{u}$ recovered in physical representation from the spectral state — the field-valued observable the iteration deposits (its cached read is vorticity_physical).

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.vorticity_physical! — Function
vorticity_physical!(ω, û, domain, spectral_scratch)

Compute the vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \nabla\times\boldsymbol{u}$ in physical representation into ω, from the velocity û in spectral representation — the field-valued observable the iteration deposits into its cache. The curl is formed as $\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{\kappa}\times\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ in spectral_scratch (a vector field in spectral representation, consumed — its coefficients are undefined on return) and transformed to physical representation by the FixedDomain domain's plans; û is only read, so unlike a pure representation recovery no preservation copy is needed. Returns ω.

The deposited value is read back with vorticity_physical, which serves the iteration's deposit or throws — measure with this function, read with that one.

Spectrum and length scale

The measured energy spectrum and the integral scale — the measured counterparts of a Phenomenology model spectrum, for validation.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.energy_spectrum — Function
energy_spectrum(û, domain)

The measured kinetic-energy spectrum of the velocity field û (in spectral representation) — the turbulent kinetic energy resolved over wavenumber-magnitude shells. What is returned is a per-shell energy $E_n$, summing to the kinetic energy, $k = \sum_n E_n$; a model spectrum $E(\kappa)$ from Phenomenology is instead a density per unit wavenumber, with $k = \int_0^\infty E(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}\kappa$, so the two differ by the shell width $\Delta\kappa = 2\pi/L$ and are not interchangeable. Returns a named tuple (; wavenumbers, energy): the shell-center wavenumber magnitudes $\kappa$ and the energy $E_n$ in each shell (positional destructuring κ, E = energy_spectrum(û, domain) also works). Both come back as host Vectors whatever backend û lives on: the shell accumulators are $O(N)$ and the reduction brings them back, so a plotting or fitting caller needs no transfer of its own. It is the GBGPeriodicFields radial spectrum of û halved — the field reduction sums $\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^2$ while the kinetic energy carries the $\tfrac{1}{2}$. The domain (a FixedDomain) supplies the wavenumber multipliers. This is the measured counterpart of a Phenomenology model spectrum, for validation.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Observables.integral_scale — Function
integral_scale(û, domain)

The longitudinal integral length scale of the velocity field û, measured from its per-shell energies $E_n$ (from energy_spectrum),

\[L_{11} = \frac{3\pi}{4} \frac{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)/\kappa\,\mathrm{d}\kappa}{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}\kappa}.\]

$L_{11}$ is the large-eddy scale — the one flow parameter a Phenomenology model spectrum needs that the small scales do not fix. The integrals above are the idealized continuous definition written with the spectral density $E(\kappa)$; they are evaluated as sums over the measured per-shell energies $E_n$, whose common shell width cancels in the ratio, so no conversion between the two is needed here. The cancellation is exact; what is approximate is the shell itself — $E_n$ is the exact sum over a shell's lattice modes, and identifying it with $E(\kappa_n)\Delta\kappa$ is a midpoint quadrature whose error is largest at the low shells, where the mode count departs most from the continuum $4\pi\kappa^2$. Those are also the shells this ratio weights most, so read $L_{11}$ as resolution-limited at coarse grids. The domain (a FixedDomain) supplies the wavenumber multipliers.

For reference, two related scales follow from the same spectrum. The transverse integral scale is $L_{22} = L_{11}/2$ (the incompressible-isotropic relation). The scale defined from the full trace velocity correlation $R_{ii}(r) = \langle\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x}+r\boldsymbol{e})\rangle$ ($\boldsymbol{e}$ a unit vector) — with no directional projection — is

\[\begin{aligned} L_E &= \frac{\pi}{2} \frac{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)/\kappa\,\mathrm{d}\kappa}{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}\kappa} \\ &= \tfrac{2}{3}L_{11} = \tfrac{1}{3}(L_{11} + 2L_{22}), \end{aligned}\]

the isotropic average of the one longitudinal and two transverse contributions.
