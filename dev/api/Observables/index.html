Observables

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables — Module
Observables

The measured functions of the flow state:

  • kinetic_energy, scalar_variance — the turbulent kinetic energy and the scalar variance.
  • dissipation, scalar_dissipation — their dissipation rates.
  • kinetic_energy_and_dissipation — $(k, \varepsilon)$ in one fused traversal.
  • kinetic_energy_and_dissipation_below_scale — the kinetic energy and dissipation held in scales below each box side $l$ the grid offers.
  • derivative_skewness — the velocity-derivative skewness, the classical marker of a developed cascade.
  • max_speed — the fastest flow speed on the grid, which sets the advective-CFL step (one generic shared with Cache: the field reduction is homed here, the cached read there).
  • vorticity_physical! — the vorticity recovered in physical representation from the state's spectral representation, the one field-valued observable (its cached read, vorticity_physical, lives in Cache).
  • energy_spectrum, integral_scale — the measured energy spectrum and the integral length scale derived from it.
  • reynolds_box, reynolds_taylor — the box and Taylor-microscale Reynolds numbers, from measured scalars rather than a field.

All but the two Reynolds numbers are functions of the state: most reduce a field to a scalar, the energy spectrum reduces to the spectral density sampled on the wavenumber shells, and the vorticity recovery alone produces a field. Each returns the field's own real element type rather than a fixed Float64, so a Float32 run measures in Float32.

Three observables gate the automatic warm-up (warmup!), which advances until the dissipation $\varepsilon$ stops drifting, the derivative skewness reaches its plateau, and — when the scalar is forced — the scalar variance settles too. The kinetic energy is not among them: a constant-energy forcing clamps it flat and a fixed-injection forcing makes it ring, so it is measured (it normalizes the eddy-turnover clock) but never gated on.

None dealiases first: the quadratic reductions are exactly alias-free under a truncating dealiasing band (see kinetic_energy for the condition; HouLi meets it only to machine precision), and the cubic skewness carries a slight aliasing residual — its docstring states the per-strategy standing (priced for the sharp truncations, unpriced for HouLi's taper).

Energy and variance

The velocity kinetic energy and its scalar analogue, the variance — both Parseval quadratic invariants.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.kinetic_energy — Function
kinetic_energy(u)
kinetic_energy(û)

The turbulent kinetic energy $\texttt{kinetic\_energy} \equiv k = \tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{u}\rvert^{2}\rangle$ of the velocity field $\boldsymbol{u}$ (the u argument). Computed as $\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts the velocity in either representation — the physical field $\boldsymbol{u}$ or its spectral coefficients $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ — to the same value.

Like every quadratic reduction here (dissipation, scalar_variance, scalar_dissipation), it needs no dealiasing of its own: the sum over spectral coefficients is exact, and the equivalent physical-space mean picks up no aliasing either, because a quadratic's zero mode folds in nothing when $2\kappa_{\max} < N$ in units of the fundamental $2\pi/L$ — satisfied by the $2/3$ rule, by PhaseShift's wider $\sqrt{2}N/3$ band, and by every narrower band.

HouLi retains the full band out to the Nyquist $N/2$, where the only pairs that fold to zero are Nyquist modes its taper has already damped to machine precision — alias-free in practice, not exactly.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.scalar_variance — Function
scalar_variance(φ)
scalar_variance(φ̂)

The scalar variance $\langle\varphi^{2}\rangle$ of the scalar field φ (for a mean-zero $\varphi$). Computed as $\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts φ in either representation.

Dissipation rates

The kinetic-energy dissipation $\varepsilon$ and the scalar-variance dissipation $\varepsilon_\varphi$.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.dissipation — Function
dissipation(ω, viscosity)
dissipation(û, domain, viscosity)

The mean kinetic-energy dissipation rate of a homogeneous flow — a single number, not a field:

\[\begin{aligned} \varepsilon &= \nu \langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle \\ &= 2\nu\langle S_{ij}S_{ij}\rangle, \end{aligned}\]

the two forms equal for a homogeneous, divergence-free flow (periodicity alone removes the boundary terms; the remaining difference is the dilatation $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}$, zero for the Leray-projected state a run advances), with the strain-rate tensor $S_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x_j}u_i + \partial_{x_i}u_j)$.

Arguments

  • ω — first form: a vorticity field, in either representation.
  • û — second form: the velocity in spectral representation.
  • domain — second form: the FixedDomain supplying the $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}$ multiplier.
  • viscosity — both forms: the kinematic viscosity $\nu$.

Two routes reach the same rate; take the one whose input is already in hand. The first form measures from the vorticity as the normalized inner product (Parseval) — use it where $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ exists for its own sake, as the cached vorticity does. The second measures straight from the velocity, with no vorticity formed at all — use it to measure off the state, where a $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ would be built only to be reduced away.

The second route trades the curl for a $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}$-weighted spectral inner product against the domain.squared_wavenumber_kernel multiplier: from

\[\begin{aligned} \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\times\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2} &= \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2} - \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2}, \end{aligned}\]

the mean-square vorticity of a divergence-free velocity is its Dirichlet energy — the mean square of its gradient, $H^1$ in the Sobolev naming — $\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle = \langle\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rangle$.

The velocity route holds only for a divergence-free field

The û route drops the $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^{2}$ term, so it holds only for a divergence-free velocity — exact for a state advanced by the Leray-projected dynamics, wrong for an arbitrary vector field. The vorticity route carries no such condition. (The scalar twin, scalar_dissipation, is unconditional: a scalar has no solenoidal constraint to invoke.)

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.scalar_dissipation — Function
scalar_dissipation(φ̂, domain, diffusivity)

The mean scalar-variance dissipation rate $\varepsilon_\varphi = 2\varkappa \langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$.

Arguments

  • φ̂ — the scalar field in spectral representation.
  • domain — the FixedDomain bundle.
  • diffusivity — the scalar diffusivity $\varkappa = \nu/\mathrm{Sc}$.

The gradient energy $\langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$ — the mean square of the scalar's gradient, the Dirichlet or $H^{1}$ energy in the Sobolev naming — equals $\langle\hat\varphi,\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}\hat\varphi\rangle$: a $\lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2}$-weighted spectral inner product against the domain.squared_wavenumber_kernel multiplier, formed straight from the spectral coefficients with no physical gradient $\nabla\varphi$ materialized.

It is the scalar twin of dissipation's spectral route, and unconditional where that one needs a divergence-free velocity. This spectral route is the only form: $\nabla\varphi$ is never recovered or cached, so there is no field-argument twin.

The fused energy–dissipation pair

Both quadratic reductions of the velocity from one traversal — the eddy-turnover clock's pair.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.kinetic_energy_and_dissipation — Function
kinetic_energy_and_dissipation(û, domain, viscosity)

Measure the turbulent kinetic energy $k$ and the viscous dissipation rate $\varepsilon$ together, from one fused traversal of the velocity in spectral representation.

The two values are kinetic_energy(û) and dissipation(û, domain, viscosity) — each formula at its own home; the dissipation is bitwise identical to its single call, the kinetic energy equal to within summation-order roundoff (a few ulp: the single reduces on a threaded tree, the fused pass serially) — but the pair costs one read of the coefficients and one blocking device-to-host readback instead of two. Reach for it wherever both are wanted at every iteration; the standing case is the eddy-turnover clock $\Delta t \varepsilon/k$ of a spin-up or budget loop, which is how StationarityCriterion measures it.

Arguments

  • û — the velocity in spectral representation, divergence-free: the dissipation is measured by the velocity route of dissipation, exact only for a solenoidal field (its docstring carries the condition).
  • domain — the FixedDomain whose lazy reduction weights the traversal evaluates.
  • viscosity — the kinematic viscosity $\nu$ scaling the dissipation.

Returns a named tuple (kinetic_energy, dissipation), each in the field's own real element type.

Energy and dissipation below a scale

The kinetic energy and the dissipation held in scales below each box side the grid offers — what a box of that side measures once its own mean is removed, averaged over every position.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.kinetic_energy_and_dissipation_below_scale — Function
kinetic_energy_and_dissipation_below_scale(û, domain, viscosity)

The kinetic energy and the dissipation rate held in scales below a box side $l$, for every side $l = nL/N$ ($n = 1, \dots, N$) of an $N^{3}$ grid on a box of side $L$.

Cut from the flow a cubic box of side $l$, remove the box's own mean velocity, and take the kinetic energy of what is left; averaged over every position of the box, that is $k(l)$. The dissipation $\varepsilon(l)$ removes the box's mean vorticity instead, since removing the mean velocity leaves every gradient unchanged.

With $\langle\boldsymbol{u}\rangle_l$ and $\langle\boldsymbol{\omega}\rangle_l$ the box means of side $l$ centred at each point (fields), and $\langle\cdot\rangle$ the average over the whole box,

\[\begin{aligned} k(l) &= k - \tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{u}\rangle_l\rvert^{2}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}} \bigl[1 - W_l(\boldsymbol{\kappa})^{2}\bigr] \lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\rvert^{2}, \\ \varepsilon(l) &= \varepsilon - \nu\langle\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\omega}\rangle_l\rvert^{2}\rangle = \nu \sum_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}} \lvert\boldsymbol{\kappa}\rvert^{2} \bigl[1 - W_l(\boldsymbol{\kappa})^{2}\bigr] \lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\rvert^{2}, \end{aligned}\]

with $W_l(\boldsymbol{\kappa}) = \prod_{a} \operatorname{sinc}(\kappa_a l/2)$ the transfer function of the box mean, the sums in the normalized convention of kinetic_energy. Both rise from zero as $l \to 0$ to $k$ and $\varepsilon$ at $l = L$, where the box is the domain and only the mean is removed. The value $k(l)$ is the position average of the residual kinetic energy of a top-hat filter of width $l$ in Pope (2000, Exercise 13.22); in the inertial range it grows as $(\varepsilon l)^{2/3}$.

The box mean is that of the band-limited field the coefficients represent, not the mean of the grid values inside the box, even for a side of a whole number of cells (the window is tophat_window). Each value is the whole-field reduction minus the retained part, so its relative precision is lower by the ratio $k/k(l)$.

Arguments

  • û — the velocity in spectral representation, divergence-free: the dissipation is measured by the velocity route of dissipation, exact only for a solenoidal field.
  • domain — the FixedDomain the velocity lives on, which fixes $N$, $L$ and the windows.
  • viscosity — the kinematic viscosity $\nu$ scaling the dissipation.

Returns a named tuple (scales, kinetic_energy, dissipation) of three host vectors of length $N$, in the field's own real element type: the sides $l$, then $k(l)$ and $\varepsilon(l)$ at each.

An occasional diagnostic, not a per-step one

Each of the $N$ sides is a weighted reduction over the $N^{3}$ coefficients of û, so the cost grows as $N^{4}$. Measure it at observations, not inside a stepping loop. In Float32 the subtraction above costs digits where $k(l) \ll k$, at the smallest sides of a field with little small-scale energy; measure in Float64 where those sides matter.

References

  • Pope, S. B. (2000). Turbulent Flows. Cambridge University Press.

Reynolds numbers

The box and Taylor-microscale Reynolds numbers, computed from measured scalars (a run's time-mean $\varepsilon$ and $k$, its $\nu$ and box side) rather than from a field.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.reynolds_box — Function
reynolds_box(side_length, dissipation, viscosity)

The box Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{box}}$ of a flow, from three scalars: the Reynolds number of an eddy the size of the box, with the Kolmogorov velocity $(\varepsilon L)^{1/3}$ of that eddy and the box side $L$,

\[\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{box}} = \frac{(\varepsilon L)^{1/3} L}{\nu} = \frac{\varepsilon^{1/3} L^{4/3}}{\nu} = \frac{1}{\tilde{\nu}},\]

with $\tilde{\nu}$ the viscosity in box units. Use it to place a run on the Reynolds axis the kinetic energy in box units $\tilde{k}$ is stated against; reynolds_taylor gives the Taylor-microscale Reynolds number of the same run. A run whose viscosity is set by the resolution $\kappa_{\max}\eta$ has $\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{box}} = (L/\eta)^{4/3}$, with $\eta$ the Kolmogorov length.

Arguments

  • side_length — the box side $L$.
  • dissipation — the dissipation rate $\varepsilon$, such as a run's measured time mean.
  • viscosity — the kinematic viscosity $\nu$.

The arguments run from the largest scale to the smallest.

Returns a number in the floating-point type the arguments promote to. A non-positive or non-finite side_length or viscosity, or a negative or non-finite dissipation, throws an ArgumentError.

source
GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.reynolds_taylor — Function
reynolds_taylor(kinetic_energy, dissipation, viscosity)

The Taylor-microscale Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}_\lambda = u'\lambda/\nu$ of a flow, from three scalars, with $u' = \sqrt{2k/3}$ the root-mean-square velocity of one component and $\lambda = \sqrt{15\nu u'^{2}/\varepsilon}$ the Taylor microscale, which gives

\[\mathrm{Re}_\lambda = \sqrt{\frac{20}{3}} \frac{k}{\sqrt{\varepsilon\nu}}.\]

It is the Reynolds number turbulence results are usually compared on. Its companion reynolds_box gives the box Reynolds number of the same run, and the two are tied by the kinetic energy in box units, $\mathrm{Re}_\lambda = \tilde{k}\sqrt{(20/3)\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{box}}}$.

Arguments

  • kinetic_energy — the turbulent kinetic energy $k$.
  • dissipation — the dissipation rate $\varepsilon$.
  • viscosity — the kinematic viscosity $\nu$.

The arguments run from the largest scale to the smallest.

Returns a number in the floating-point type the arguments promote to. A negative or non-finite kinetic_energy, or a non-positive or non-finite dissipation or viscosity, throws an ArgumentError.

Derivative skewness

The velocity-derivative skewness — the developed-turbulence marker that gates warm-up.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.derivative_skewness — Function
derivative_skewness(û, domain)

The velocity-derivative skewness $S$, written coordinate-free as the normalized third invariant of the strain-rate tensor $\boldsymbol{S} = \operatorname{sym}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}$ (the italic $S$ is the scalar skewness, the bold $\boldsymbol{S}$ the strain-rate tensor):

\[\begin{aligned} S &= C_S \frac{\langle\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle^{3/2}} = 3C_S \frac{\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle^{3/2}}, \\ C_S &= \tfrac{4}{7}\sqrt{15/2} \approx 1.5649. \end{aligned}\]

Because $\boldsymbol{S}$ is symmetric and, for the incompressible field, traceless, $\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3} = 3\det\boldsymbol{S}$. By isotropy this equals the classical longitudinal skewness $\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{3}\rangle / \langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{2}\rangle^{3/2}$; the constant follows from $\langle\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{S}^{3}\rangle = \tfrac{105}{8}\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{3}\rangle$ and $\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle = \tfrac{15}{2}\langle(\partial_{x_1} u_1)^{2}\rangle$ (Betchov 1956). So the reported value is the familiar $S \approx -0.5$ of developed turbulence, but from a genuine tensor invariant rather than an axis-dependent gradient.

From û in spectral representation, the strain is formed with symmetric_gradient and the wavenumber multipliers of the FixedDomain bundle domain, skipping the full velocity gradient, and carried to physical space through the domain's transform plans; domain also sizes this per-call tensor scratch. A fused mapped mean reduces $\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ with no determinant field, and a field with no strain returns zero.

The denominator needs no physical field: for a solenoidal field $\langle\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle$, the Dirichlet energy, reduced from û.

Because $\langle\det\boldsymbol{S}\rangle$ is a cubic invariant, its grid mean carries a small residual aliasing on a $2/3$-truncated field — negligible for a well-resolved run, but, unlike the quadratic reductions, not exactly alias-free. That pricing is the sharp-truncation one: the wider PhaseShift ball carries the same kind of residual over its larger retained band, and HouLi's smooth taper leaves a residual of its own shape that no bound here prices — on every strategy, treat the skewness as an indicator, not an alias-controlled measurement.

The skewness $S$ is the classic marker of developed turbulence: a random-phase (Gaussian) field has $S \approx 0$ (all odd invariants vanish), while fully developed isotropic turbulence has $S \approx -0.5$ (an empirical, weakly Reynolds-number-dependent value) — the enstrophy-production / vortex-stretching signature. Its plateau is the "real turbulence yet" gate of the warm-up detector.

This is the run's largest transient allocation

The rank-2 strain tensor is allocated per call rather than kept resident — an occasional diagnostic should not hold field-scale storage for a whole run — but while it lives it is the biggest single memory episode a default configuration reaches, larger than the resident working set. The warmup! driver calls this on its sampling interval when its skewness gate is on (gate_skewness = true; off by default), so a run near its memory ceiling should size for the diagnostic rather than for the steady state.

References

  • Betchov, R. (1956). An inequality concerning the production of vorticity in isotropic turbulence. J. Fluid Mech. **1*

Timestep control

The fastest flow speed on the grid, which sets the advective-CFL step — a reduction, not a physics invariant.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.max_speed — Function
max_speed(u)
max_speed(cache)

The fastest flow speed on the grid, $\max_{\boldsymbol{x}}\lvert\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x})\rvert$, of the velocity field u in physical representation — the speed that sets the advective-CFL timestep. The magnitude is formed one grid point at a time and consumed by the maximum immediately, so no magnitude field is materialized.

The second form reads the number back from an ObservablesCache, where the closing deposit left it — the same quantity, measured once per iteration (a step plus the deposit that closes it) and served without a reduction (or a device readback); it throws an ErrorException when no deposit is current, like every cached read.

A grid maximum, not a continuum supremum

The reduction runs over grid points, so between them the flow can be faster than any sampled value, which makes the sampled maximum an under-estimate — one of the two loosenesses recommended_cfl prices when it sets its margin. A CFL condition bounds the stability of the explicit advective term; it is not by itself a guarantee of accuracy.

Why this observable alone has a cached form

The cache holds exactly what the iteration already computes and leaves alive at its end; its contents follow from the run's fixed computation schedule. The maximum speed qualifies because the run itself needs it: on the adaptive-CFL path the next timestep is chosen from it, so the reduction happens whether or not anyone reads the value. Under a hand-fixed $\Delta t$ nothing consumes it and the pass is spent anyway — the schedule is fixed when the cache is built and does not vary with how the run is driven. The observables kinetic_energy and dissipation have no cached form because no part of the iteration leaves one alive at its end; caching either would mean adding a reduction the run does not otherwise perform, which is the one thing the cache may not do.

Vorticity recovery

The vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \nabla\times\boldsymbol{u}$ recovered in physical representation from the state's spectral representation — the field-valued observable the iteration deposits (its cached read is vorticity_physical).

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.vorticity_physical! — Function
vorticity_physical!(ω, û, domain, spectral_scratch)

Compute the vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \nabla\times\boldsymbol{u}$ in physical representation into ω — the field-valued observable the iteration deposits into its cache. The curl is formed as $\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{\kappa}\times\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ in spectral representation and transformed to physical.

Arguments

  • ω — the vector field in physical representation receiving the vorticity.
  • û — the velocity in spectral representation; only read, so unlike a pure representation recovery no preservation copy is needed.
  • domain — the FixedDomain whose plans transform.
  • spectral_scratch — a vector field in spectral representation the curl is formed in; consumed — its coefficients are undefined on return.

Returns ω.

The deposited value is read back with vorticity_physical, which serves the iteration's deposit or throws — measure with this function, read with that one.

Spectrum and length scale

The measured energy spectrum and the integral scale — the measured counterparts of a Phenomenology model spectrum, for validation.

GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.energy_spectrum — Function
energy_spectrum(û, domain)

The measured kinetic-energy spectral density $E(\kappa)$ of the velocity field û (in spectral representation): $E(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}\kappa$ is the turbulent kinetic energy carried by wavenumber magnitudes in $[\kappa, \kappa + \mathrm{d}\kappa]$, so $k = \int_0^\infty E(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}\kappa$ — the object the reference laws are stated about, directly comparable to a Phenomenology model spectrum with no conversion. The domain (a FixedDomain) supplies the wavenumber multipliers and the shell width.

Returns a named tuple (; wavenumbers, energy): the shell-center wavenumber magnitudes $\kappa$ and the density estimate at each (positional destructuring κ, E = energy_spectrum(û, domain) also works). Both come back as host Vectors whatever backend û lives on — the $O(N)$ shell accumulators return with the reduction, so a caller needs no transfer of its own.

Each value is the shell's energy — half the GBGPeriodicFields radial spectrum, which sums $\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\rvert^2$ — divided by the shell width $\Delta\kappa = 2\pi/L$, making Parseval the quadrature $k = \sum_n E(\kappa_n) \Delta\kappa$ (exact by construction). At low $\kappa$ the integer shells hold few lattice modes, so the density estimate is correspondingly lumpy there.

source
GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.Observables.integral_scale — Function
integral_scale(û, domain)

The longitudinal integral length scale of the velocity field û, measured from its spectral density (from energy_spectrum),

\[L_{11} = \frac{3\pi}{4} \frac{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)/\kappa\,\mathrm{d}\kappa}{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}\kappa}.\]

The integral scale $L_{11}$ is the large-eddy scale — the one flow parameter a Phenomenology model spectrum needs that the small scales do not fix. The domain (a FixedDomain) supplies the wavenumber multipliers.

The integrals above are the idealized continuous definition, written with the spectral density $E(\kappa)$ that energy_spectrum measures; they are evaluated as quadrature sums over the shells, the common shell width canceling in the ratio.

The cancellation is exact; what is approximate is the shell itself: $E_n$ is the exact sum over a shell's lattice modes, and identifying it with $E(\kappa_n)\Delta\kappa$ is a midpoint quadrature whose error is largest at the low shells, where the mode count departs most from the continuum $4\pi\kappa^2$. Those are also the shells this ratio weights most, so read $L_{11}$ as resolution-limited at coarse grids.

For reference, two related scales follow from the same spectrum. The transverse integral scale is $L_{22} = L_{11}/2$ (the incompressible-isotropic relation). The scale defined from the full trace velocity correlation $R_{ii}(r) = \langle\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x}+r\boldsymbol{e})\rangle$ ($\boldsymbol{e}$ a unit vector) — with no directional projection — is

\[\begin{aligned} L_E &= \frac{\pi}{2} \frac{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)/\kappa\,\mathrm{d}\kappa}{\int_0^\infty E(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}\kappa} \\ &= \tfrac{2}{3}L_{11} = \tfrac{1}{3}(L_{11} + 2L_{22}), \end{aligned}\]

the isotropic average of the one longitudinal and two transverse contributions.
