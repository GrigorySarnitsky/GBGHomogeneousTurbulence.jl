Equations

The incompressible Navier–Stokes right-hand side in rotational form — the de-aliased, Leray-projected nonlinear term and the exact viscous integrating factor — together with the velocity diagnostics (kinetic energy, dissipation, maximum speed).

Public API

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.derivative_skewness — Method
derivative_skewness(uh, ops, ws)

The velocity-derivative skewness

\[S = \frac{\big\langle(\partial_{x_i} u_i)^3\big\rangle}{\big\langle(\partial_{x_i} u_i)^2\big\rangle^{3/2}},\]

averaged over the three diagonal components $i = 1,2,3$ for isotropy, of the velocity uh (given in spectral representation). Each diagonal derivative $\partial_{x_i} u_i$ is formed spectrally (gradient!), transformed to physical space, and reduced to its second and third moments.

$S$ is the classic marker of developed turbulence: a random-phase (Gaussian) field has $S \approx 0$ (a linear image of a Gaussian is Gaussian, hence symmetric), while fully developed isotropic turbulence has $S \approx -0.5$ (Kolmogorov). Its plateau is the strict "real turbulence yet" gate of the warm-up detector. No field-sized allocation — reuses the workspace scratch (ws.scratch, ws.velocity_star).

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.dissipation — Method
dissipation(vorticity, viscosity)

The mean kinetic-energy dissipation rate of a homogeneous flow, $\varepsilon = \nu\,\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle$, from the physical vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and the kinematic viscosity $\nu$ (the homogeneous identity $\varepsilon = 2\nu\langle S_{ij}S_{ij}\rangle = \nu\langle\lvert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rvert^{2}\rangle$).

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.integrating_factor! — Function
integrating_factor!(factor, ops, diffusivity, dt, rate = nothing)

Write the exact diffusive integrating factor into the scalar spectral buffer factor. Over a step $\Delta t$ the diffusion equation $\partial_t f = D\nabla^{2} f$ is solved exactly in spectral representation by the per-mode multiplier

\[\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{k}) = \exp\!\big(-D\,\lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert^{2}\,\Delta t\big),\]

so multiplying a field by factor advances its viscous/diffusive part exactly (no stability restriction). The diffusivity $D$ (diffusivity) is the kinematic viscosity $\nu$ for the velocity and $\nu/\mathrm{Sc}$ for the scalar $\varphi$ ($\mathrm{Sc}$ the Schmidt number).

When a linear forcing rate $A(\mathbf{k})$ is supplied (rate, a real array; from the forcing_rate! of a Linear/ConstantPower forcing), it is folded into the linear operator, $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{k}) = \exp\!\big((A(\mathbf{k}) - D\lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert^{2})\,\Delta t\big)$ — exact for the linear forcing, so the integrator keeps its order.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.kinetic_energy — Method
kinetic_energy(velocity)

The turbulent kinetic energy $\tfrac12\langle\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert^{2}\rangle$ of the velocity field velocity. Computed as $\tfrac12\,\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts velocity in either representation.

source
GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.max_speed — Method
max_speed(velocity)

The largest velocity component in absolute value, $\max_i\lvert u_i\rvert$ — an allocation-free reduction used to set the advective CFL timestep. It differs from the true $\sup\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert$ by at most $\sqrt{3}$, well inside the CFL margin.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.scalar_dissipation — Method
scalar_dissipation(scalar_gradient, diffusivity)

The mean scalar-variance dissipation rate $\chi = 2\kappa\,\langle\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\rangle$, from the physical scalar gradient scalar_gradient $\nabla\varphi$ and the scalar diffusivity $\kappa = \nu/\mathrm{Sc}$.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.scalar_nonlinear! — Method
scalar_nonlinear!(nonlinear, velocity, scalar, ops, ws)

Assemble the de-aliased advection term of the passive-scalar equation into nonlinear, from the physical velocity $\mathbf{u}$ and physical scalar $\varphi$.

In conservative form the advection is $-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{u}\varphi)$ (equal to $-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi$ since $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0$); the conservative form is used because, with the $2/3$ rule, it conserves the scalar mean exactly (the divergence has no $\mathbf{k}=\mathbf 0$ component). It is computed pseudospectrally as $-\,\mathrm{dealias}\big(i\mathbf{k}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{u}\varphi}\big)$. The scalar flux $\mathbf{u}\varphi$ reuses the physical and spectral velocity scratch.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.scalar_variance — Method
scalar_variance(scalar)

The scalar variance $\langle\varphi^{2}\rangle$ of the scalar field scalar (for a mean-zero $\varphi$). Computed as $\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle$ with the normalized inner product, so by Parseval's theorem it accepts scalar in either representation.

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.velocity_nonlinear! — Method
velocity_nonlinear!(nonlinear, velocity, vorticity, ops, ws)

Assemble the de-aliased, Leray-projected nonlinear term of the momentum equation into nonlinear, from the physical velocity $\mathbf{u}$ and vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega}$.

In physical space the rotational form writes the convective term as the Lamb vector: with the identity $(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u} = \nabla\big(\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert^{2}\big) - \mathbf{u}\times\boldsymbol{\omega}$, the divergence-free momentum equation becomes $\partial_t\mathbf{u} = \mathbb{P}\,(\mathbf{u}\times\boldsymbol{\omega}) + \nu\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{f}$, where the Leray–Helmholtz projection $\mathbb{P}$ onto divergence-free fields absorbs the pressure and the $\nabla(\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert^{2})$ gradient. The term computed here is the spectral image of $\mathbb{P}\,(\mathbf{u}\times\boldsymbol{\omega})$, with the $2/3$ rule removing the aliasing of the quadratic product. Allocation-free (a destination distinct from its sources takes the no-alias path).

GBGIsotropicTurbulence.Equations.velocity_vorticity! — Method
velocity_vorticity!(velocity, vorticity, uh, ops, ws)

Compute the physical velocity $\mathbf{u}$ and vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \nabla\times\mathbf{u}$ of the velocity field uh (given in spectral representation $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$) into the buffers velocity and vorticity.

uh is preserved: the vorticity is transformed first, then a copy of uh is transformed — the in-place inverse transform consumes its spectral source.
